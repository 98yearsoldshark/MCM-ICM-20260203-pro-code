# 2. Assumptions and Justifications

In order to simplify the problem and improve the model's tractability and computationalefficiency, we make the following assumptions

• Assumption 1: Assuming the use of lithium-ion batteries.

Justifications 1: Lithium-ion batteries represent the mainstream power solution in thesmartphone sector, offering significant advantages in high energy density and long cycle life.Establishing a unified chemical framework facilitates standardized modeling using knownelectrochemical parameters.

• Assumption 2: Assumes the battery state changes continuously over time.

Justifications 2: Battery charging and discharging constitute a macroscopic electrochemicalreaction process without abrupt jumps. Based on this assumption, we can employ OrdinaryDifferential Equations (ODEs) to precisely describe the trajectory of the battery state's dynamicevolution over time.

• Assumption 3: Battery capacity gradually degrades with increasing charge-discharge cycles.

Justifications 3: This inherent physical characteristic of lithium-ion batteries enables the modelto reflect not only short-term capacity changes but also assess long-term device health.

• Assumption 4: Transitions between high and low operating states are calculated asinfinitesimally small time intervals.

Justifications 4: Although microsecond-level physical delays exist in hardware circuit voltageand current stabilization, these transition times are negligible relative to the battery's dischargecycles spanning several hours, allowing for simplified model analysis.

• Assumption 5: Assume power consumption of each hardware component is independent.

Justifications 5: This simplifies model complexity, enhancing practical applicability in real-world scenarios.

# Abstract

The escalating demand for efficient thermal management in lithium-ion batteries necessitates precise characterization of their thermal behavior under diverse operating conditionsThis study develops a three-dimensional (3D) electrochemical-thermal coupling modelgrounded in porous electrode theory and energy conservation principles. The model solvesmulti-physics equations such as Fick's law, Ohm's law, and the Butler-Volmer equation,to resolve coupled electrochemical and thermal dynamics, with temperature-dependentparameters calibrated via the Arrhenius equation. Simulations under varying dischargerates reveal that high-rate discharges exacerbate internal heat accumulation. Low ambient temperatures amplify polarization effects. Forced convection cooling reduces surfacetemperatures but exacerbates core-to-surface thermal gradients. Structural optimizationstrategies demonstrate that enhancing through-thickness thermal conductivity reducestemperature diferences. These findings underscore the necesity of balancing energy density and thermal management in lithium-ion batery design, proposing actionable insightssuch as preheating protocols for low-temperature operation,optimized cooling systems forhigh-rate scenarios, and material-level enhancements for improved thermal uniformity.

# 5. Model 1：Continuous-time model for battery state ofcharge

5.1 The Establishment of Model 1

Based on a physical abstraction of smartphone power supply systems, this study addresses thelimitation of traditional discrete fitting models in explaining "unpredictable power depletion" bytreating smartphones as multi-field coupled open thermodynamic systems. We construct aHybrid Dynamical System that deeply couples the battery's electrochemical reactions andthermodynamic evolution with discrete events at the upper system layer (user behavior,component state machines). Unlike conventional power consumption superposition models,this approach precisely captures the "synergistic effects" and "unpredictability" during batterydepletion by integrating a finite state machine (FSM) power layer with continuous-timedifferential equations.

# Abstract

 The escalating demand for efficient thermal management in lithium-ion batteries necessi- tates precise characterization of their thermal behavior under diverse operating conditions. This study develops a three-dimensional (3D) electrochemical-thermal coupling model grounded in porous electrode theory and energy conservation principles. The model solves multi-physics equations such as Fick's law, Ohm's law, and the Butler-Volmer equation,to resolve coupled electrochemical and thermal dynamics, with temperature-dependent parameters calibrated via the Arrhenius equation. Simulations under varying discharge rates reveal that high-rate discharges exacerbate internal heat accumulation. Low ambi- ent temperatures amplify polarization effects. Forced convection cooling reduces surface temperatures but exacerbates core-to-surface thermal gradients. Structural optimization strategies demonstrate that enhancing through-thickness thermal conductivity reduces temperature differences. These findings underscore the necessity of balancing energy den- sity and thermal management in lithium-ion battery design, proposing actionable insights such as preheating protocols for low-temperature operation, optimized cooling systems for high-rate scenarios, and material-level enhancements for improved thermal uniformity.
