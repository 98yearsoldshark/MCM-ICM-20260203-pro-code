# 4.1.1 Basic Differential Equation Derivation

First, we define the battery State of Charge (SOC) as the percentage of the currentremaining charge relative to the rated capacity:

$$
S O C (t) = \frac {Q (t)}{Q _ {\mathrm {eff}} (t)} \times 100 \%
$$

Where:

Q(t) is the remaining charge at time t(unit: mAh).

$Q _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ( t ) .$ is the total battery capacity (unit: mAh).

At ${ \mathrm { S O C } } = 1$ , the battery is fully charged; at ${ \mathrm { S O C } } = 0$ , the system considers thebattery "depleted".

At the same time, lithium-ion batteries have an inherent characteristic: their capacitygradually degrades with increasing number of charge cycles. Therefore, when designing thebattery model, we also factor in this characteristic.

$$
\begin{array}{l} C _ {t} h \frac {d T}{d t} = I (t) ^ {2} R 0 (S O C, T, S O H) + \alpha_ {c} h i p P _ {c} h i p (t) - h A (T - T _ {a} m b (t)) \\ V _ {\text {t e r m}} (t) = O C V \left(S O C, T\right) - I \cdot R 0 \left(S O C, T, S O H\right) - U 1 - U 2 \\ \end{array}
$$

We define the effective available capacity of the battery $Q _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ( t )$ as a function of state ofcharge, expressed as follows:

$$
Q _ {\text {e f f}} (t) = Q _ {0} \cdot f _ {T} (T (t)) \cdot f _ {C} (I (t)) \cdot f _ {A} (A)
$$

As shown, the effective capacity is highly dependent on the nominal capacity of a newbattery under standard conditions, the temperature correction function , the battery or celltemperature , the discharge rate/current impact function , the aging (SOH) impact function ,and aging indicators such as cycle count .

Substituting the definition of SOC into the above equation yields the rate of change ofSOC:

$$
\frac {d (S O C (t))}{d t} = \frac {1}{Q _ {\mathrm {eff}} (t)} \times \frac {d Q (t)}{d t} \times 100 \% = \frac {- I (t)}{Q _ {\mathrm {eff}} (t)} \times 100 \%
$$

$I ( t ) { = } \frac { P _ { \mathrm { b a t t } } ( t ) } { \eta ( t ) \ { V } _ { \mathrm { b a t } } ( t ) } \mathrm { T o }$ simplify the model, using mAh/h as the power consumption unitallows $I ( t )$ to be converted into the power P we wish to study. Therefore, the finalfundamental differential equation is:

$$
\frac {d S O C}{d t} = \frac {- P}{Q _ {\mathrm {eff}} (t)} \times 100 \%
$$

This equation describes the relationship between the rate of SOC change over time andpower consumption: as power consumption increases, the SOC decline accelerates; as batterycapacity increases, the SOC decline slows down.

# 4.2.1 Discharge cut-off voltage setting

First, considering that in reality, the cut-off voltage of lithium-ion batteries significantlyimpacts factors such as "available capacity" and "lifespan/safety margin," this paper assumesa conservative strategy aligned with smartphone systems (avoiding deep discharge to preventlifespan degradation and shutdown uncertainty): Vcut $= 3 . 3$

Simultaneously, sensitivity analysis will perturb Vcut as a key parameter (e.g., 3.2–3.4V) to evaluate its impact on TTE and undervoltage probability, thereby explicitly revealingthe trade-off between "cut-off voltage selection—endurance—lifespan."

In real-world smartphone usage, prolonged operation can lead to overheating and rapidbattery drain, while low outdoor temperatures may also cause accelerated power loss. While abattery's energy storage capacity is fixed, its "available energy" is dynamically constrained byenvironmental factors and its own thermal state.

For instance, in high-temperature scenarios (such as running demanding games), intenseheat generated by the SoC chip is conducted through the device body to the battery, causingits temperature $R _ { 0 }$ to rise. While this short-term heating slightly reduces the internalresistance $R _ { 0 }$ , the overall internal resistance still increases, generating more heat.

Consequently, the power $P _ { b a t t }$ required to maintain the same functionality is forced to rise.Conversely, in low-temperature environments (e.g., cold outdoor conditions), $R _ { 0 }$ increasesexponentially. To maintain the same output power under energy conservation, the batterymust deliver a significantly higher current I . This substantial current not only acceleratesSOC depletion but also causes the terminal voltage $V _ { t e r m }$ to prematurely reach the critical $V _ { c u t }$threshold due to the sharp increase in the voltage drop $I ^ { . } R _ { 0 }$ . This results in the so-called"sudden power drop" phenomenon.

To unify these complex physical variables within an energy balance framework, weestablish the following battery-side current solution model:

We define the battery's effective open-circuit voltage $V _ { o c }$ . This is not simply the open-circuit voltage OCV , but rather the result after subtracting the voltage drops $U _ { 1 }$ $U _ { 1 } \mathrm { a n d } U _ { 2 }$ causedby polarization effects. This reflects the battery's "fatigue effect"—after operating at highcurrent for a period, the usable voltage temporarily decreases. Terminal voltage $V _ { t e r m }$ : This is

the voltage actually sensed by the phone's circuitry. When this value drops to your set cutoffvoltage $\boldsymbol { { \cdot } } \boldsymbol { { V } } _ { c u t }$ (3.3V), the system will force a shutdown.

Phone components (screen, CPU, 5G module, etc.) require power to operate.

$$
V _ {o c} = O C V (S O C, T) - U 1 - U 2, V _ {t e r m} = V _ {o c} - I \cdot R _ {0}
$$

$$
P _ {b a t t} = V _ {t e r m} \cdot I = V _ {o c} I - R _ {0} I ^ {2}
$$

The $R _ { 0 } I ^ { 2 }$ term represents internal resistance thermal losses.

$R _ { 0 } { = } R _ { 0 } \big ( S O C ( t ) , T ( t ) , S O H \big )$ This is a variable related to SOH.

This means that a portion of the electrical energy output by the battery is directlyconverted into heat within the battery itself, rather than being supplied to the phone.This explains why the battery itself heats up significantly under heavy loads.

To determine whether the battery has reached its limit, we set $\Delta ( t )$ to assess itspower consumption state.

$$
\Delta (t) = V _ {o c} ^ {2} - 4 R _ {0} P _ {b a t t}
$$

When:

$\Delta \left( t \right) > 0$ :The battery can meet the load power demand by adjusting its outputcurrent, ensuring normal system operation.

$\Delta ( t ) { = } 0$ :The system has reached its maximum power transfer point. At this point,any slight increase in load or voltage fluctuation will cause current to becomeuncontrollable.

$\Delta ( t ) { < } 0 : \mathrm { A }$ physical "voltage collapse" occurs. This typically happens when thebattery is extremely low on charge or in extremely cold environments, whereexcessive internal resistance prevents the battery from maintaining the minimumsystem operating voltage.

In the algorithm, once $\Delta \left( t \right) < 0$ the model immediately triggers the TTE terminationsignal, indicating a forced shutdown.

# 4.2.2 Time to First Termination. (non-regression)

This model defines endurance time not as a linear regression based on historical data,but as a first-time-to-reach (TTE) problem within the state space of a dynamic system. Wedeconstruct shutdown behavior as a physical-level "boundary game": When terminal voltagetouches the protection thresholdV cut, chemical energy SOC is depleted, or load demand

exceeds the battery's power output limit $( \varDelta ( t ) < 0 )$ , the system equilibrium breaks, triggering atermination signal. This modeling approach enables us to precisely capture nonlinear power-down behavior under extreme conditions, providing a scientifically grounded physicalexplanation for 'unpredictable depletion'.

Regarding the shutdown phenomenon, we have categorized it into three distincttriggering causes.

Voltage threshold trigger: $V _ { \mathrm { \it t e r m } } ( t ) \leq V _ { \mathrm { \it c u t } }$ ：

This is the most common cause of shutdown. To protect lithium batteries from over-discharge

 Energy depletion trigger: $S O C \left( t \right) \le S O C _ { m i n }$

This represents ideal state depletion.

 Power unfeasibility trigger (voltage collapse): $\Delta \left( t \right) < 0$ ：

This represents ideal state depletion.

This is the core explanation for "unexpected shutdowns." During extreme cold(where internal resistance $R _ { 0 }$ surges) or intensive gaming (where power $P _ { b a t t }$ peaks),the discriminant suddenly drops below zero. At this point, the battery can no longerdeliver sufficient current to sustain the load—both mathematically and physically—resulting in a "voltage collapse." This is the model's key to explaining "why thescreen suddenly blacks out with $1 0 \%$ battery remaining."

Analysis of Specific Causes for Rapid Battery Drain in Various Scenarios

• S1 Standby/Light Use: Devices remain largely inactive with minimal background tasksand data throughput. Battery life is primarily constrained by baseline power consumption.Extremely low current draw renders voltage loss negligible, enabling maximum endurance.

• S2 Browsing/Social Media: Primary drain stems from high-frequency screen refreshesand frequent interactive loading. As the phone's most power-hungry component, screenbrightness combined with frequent processor pulse loads constitute major energy loss.

• S3 Video/Live Streaming: Core drivers are sustained network throughput and steadyheat accumulation from hardware decoding. Prolonged high-load operation may triggersystem overheating protection, forcing power management strategy adjustments.

• S4 Gaming (Fastest Drain Scenario): CPU/GPU operating at full load generatesmassive current, causing severe internal resistance heating. This not only accelerates SOCdepletion but also causes a sharp drop in terminal voltage, posing a physical "voltagecollapse" risk.

• S5 Navigation/Calls: Driven by constantly active GPS, cellular communication, andweak-signal penalties. In complex environments, communication modules must increasetransmission power to maintain connectivity. This "signal quality degradation" is the primarydriver of rapid battery drain outdoors.

# 5.4 Prediction Results and Analysis

Factors contributing to a significant reduction in time

• Heavy computational load: Gaming scenarios (S4) cause CPU and GPU to operate athigh speeds, resulting in only approximately 3.4 hours of full-charge runtime.

• Extreme cold: Internal resistance surges at $5 \mathrm { ^ \circ F } _ { : }$ , causing voltage to drop prematurelybelow the threshold and triggering "battery collapse."

• Screen and base power consumption: The display is the primary power drain, whilebackground "base power consumption" impacts standby endurance even more than the CPU.

• Communication Loss: Under weak signal conditions (S5), the communication moduleis forced to increase power, causing rapid battery depletion.

# Unexpected: Less influential factors

• GPU/GPS Outside Specific Scenarios: When not gaming or navigating, these modulescontribute far less to daily battery fluctuation than the CPU and screen.

• Moderate heat: Battery life at $1 0 0 . 4 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { F } ,$ is even slightly better than at room temperature,as higher temperatures reduce internal resistance and enhance ion activity.

• Low-battery "endurance": Model optimization slows the decline rate in low-chargezones, creating a perception of longer endurance than during high-load usage.

# 6 Sensitivity Analysis

模型的分析 ：在建模比赛中模型分析主要有两种，一个是灵敏度(性)分析，另一个是误差分析。灵敏度分析是研究与分析一个系统（或模型）的状态或输出变化对系统参数或周围条件变化的敏感程度的方法。其通用的步骤是：控制其他参数不变的情况下，改变模型中某个重要参数的值，然后观察模型的结果的变化情况。误差分析是指分析模型中的误差来源，或者估算模型中存在的误差，一般用于预测问题或者数值计算类问题。

模型的检验：模型检验可以分为两种，一种是使用模型之前应该进行的检验，例如层次分析法中一致性检验，灰色预测中的准指数规律的检验，这部分内容应该放在模型的建立部分；另一种是使用了模型后对模型的结果进行检验，数模中最常见的是

稳定性检验，实际上这里的稳定性检验和前面的灵敏度分析非常类似，等会大家看到例子就明白了。

在美赛的写作中，写的最多的就是灵敏度分析（Sensitivity Analysis），因此这里我们的标题就直接取得是灵敏度分析；如果你既要写灵敏度分析，又要写误差分析（Error Analysis），那么你可以把标题改成： Sensitivity Analysis and Error Analysis

# 7 Model Evaluation and Further Discussion

# 7.1 Strengths

√ High Physical Fidelity: Unlike simple linear regression, our model utilizes a second-order Thevenin equivalent circuit coupled with a thermal ODE, capturing complexelectrochemical phenomena such as polarization effects and internal resistancetemperature sensitivity.

Superior "Sudden Death" Prediction: By introducing the voltage cut-off threshold ($V _ { c u t } )$ and power unfeasibility discriminant $\left( \varDelta t { < } 0 \right)$ , the model successfully explains andpredicts non-linear "unexpected shutdowns" under heavy loads or cold environments.

√ Comprehensive Multi-Dimensional Analysis: Our framework integrates discrete userbehavior events with continuous physical states, providing a holistic view of howsoftware scheduling, hardware load, and ambient factors synergistically affect TTE.

 High Generalizability: The model’s parameter-driven design allows it to adapt tovarious smartphone brands and battery chemistries simply by adjusting the inputspecification table.

# 7.2 Weaknesses

 Constant Thermal Coefficient Assumption: For computational efficiency, we assumeda constant thermal coupling coefficient $\eta$ , which may slightly overlook the dynamic heatdissipation changes caused by different phone casing materials or external airflow.

 Simplified Background Task Modeling: While we accounted for background residenttasks, the model treats them as steady-state segmented processes, which may not fullycapture the extreme stochasticity of erratic "rogue" background apps
